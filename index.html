Regional Intelligence

AI in insurance
around the world.

AI adoption in insurance is not uniform. Regulatory environments, market structures, and carrier sophistication vary significantly across regions — shaping what's Signal in one market and Noise in another.

🌏
APAC
Asia · Pacific · ANZ · South-East Asia
APAC leads in mobile-first insurance, parametric microinsurance, and AI-driven distribution. China's insurance technology ecosystem is the most advanced globally. Japan and South Korea are accelerating AI underwriting adoption. Australia and Singapore lead on regulatory sandboxes.
China: Ping An's AI underwriting processed 80M+ applications in 2024
Singapore MAS sandbox enabling AI-first insurer models
Parametric microinsurance growing 35%+ YoY across South-East Asia
🌍
Europe
UK · EU27 · EEA · Lloyd's Market
Europe leads on regulation (EU AI Act, EIOPA guidelines, FCA principles) and explainability requirements. Lloyd's of London is a major AI innovation hub for specialty lines. Continental carriers are investing heavily in climate AI for cat modelling. GDPR shapes data strategy throughout.
EU AI Act creates compliance requirements for high-risk AI use in insurance
Lloyd's Future Lab backing AI-native specialty coverage models
Aviva, Allianz deploying LLMs across customer service at scale
🌎
Americas
US · Canada · Latin America · Bermuda
The US is the largest market with the deepest AI adoption — particularly in P&C claims automation, life underwriting, and group benefits. NAIC is developing AI model law guidance. Bermuda leads in cat model innovation and ILS. Latin America is an emerging market for embedded insurance and parametric products.
NAIC model AI bulletin adopted by 15+ states through 2024
US P&C claims automation: 40-60% straight-through processing at leading carriers
Bermuda: AI-driven retrocession pricing becoming standard practice
Meet the AI Agents

How it works

Not a newsletter.
A multi-agent system.

Every Monday, eight specialist AI agents run a two-phase intelligence operation — autonomously searching the live web, challenging each other's findings, and producing a verified digest. Here is exactly what happens.

Phase 1 — 06:00 UTC Monday
Five primary agents search independently
Scout, Vita, Lex, Terra, and Horizon each receive a brief describing their domain and last week’s top signals.
Each agent autonomously generates its own search queries — deciding what is worth investigating this week — then retrieves live web results in real time. Each agent reads its results and produces 3–6 structured findings: a title, a 2–3 sentence analysis, a verdict, a confidence score (1–5), a Technology Readiness Level (1–9), a regulatory risk assessment, a research direction worth exploring, and real source URLs.
Scout — P&C Vita — Life & Annuities Lex — Regulatory Terra — Reinsurance Horizon — Horizontal Tech
Phase 2 — 06:02 UTC Monday
Three synthesis agents read everything, then search further
Two minutes after Phase 1 completes, Null, Weave, and Faro receive the full set of Phase 1 findings as context.
Each then generates its own targeted search queries — informed by what the primary agents found — and fetches additional live results. Null and Weave run in a deeper reasoning mode: a multi-step analytical process that runs before producing output. This is what separates a sceptical challenge from a surface-level dismissal, and a second-order effect from an obvious observation.
Null — Sceptic (deep reasoning) Weave — Second-order effects (deep reasoning) Faro — Horizon scanner
🔗
Every source URL is verified
Before any finding is stored, all reference URLs are HTTP-checked. Dead links are removed. If no live sources remain, the finding is flagged. Every finding shown here has at least one live, reachable source.
Live URL check on every run
🔍
Agents decide what to search
There are no pre-written queries. Each agent reads its brief and last week’s signals, then generates its own search terms. This means the system adapts to what is actually happening in the market — not what was anticipated when it was built.
Autonomous query generation
🧠
Null is always looking for the lie
Null reads every Phase 1 finding with explicit instructions to find vendor marketing dressed as innovation, thin evidence presented as proof, and AI-washing. It uses extended reasoning to do this properly — not a surface-level scan. Noise verdicts are Null’s primary output.
Active hallucination guard
📈
Signal status tracks week over week
Every finding is assigned a signal status: New, Emerging, Confirmed, or Recurring. Agents compare this week’s findings against prior weeks to detect whether a signal is strengthening, plateauing, or fading. A finding that appears once is interesting. One that appears three weeks running is a trend.
Memory across runs
🕐
Live web results, not cached data
Every search call goes to the live web at the moment the agent runs. There is no pre-fetched dataset, no static corpus, no knowledge cutoff. If something was published this morning and is relevant to insurance AI, an agent can find it by Monday.
Real-time web search
🎯
Five scoring dimensions per finding
Every finding carries: a verdict (Signal / Watch / Noise), a confidence score (1–5), a Technology Readiness Level (1–9 per NASA/ESA standard), a regulatory risk rating (Low / Medium / High), and a research direction worth exploring. Scoring is not binary.
